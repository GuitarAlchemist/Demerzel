Expected answers, copied from `results.json` under `transfer_task` (a hypothetical team of 4 people, computed from `scenarios.json`):

- Question 1: R0 = 0.5/0.25 = 2. At the start R0 × S/N = 2 × 3/4 = 3/2 = 1.5, above the threshold. Holders rise while the unaware people exceed N/R0 = 2; there are 3 at the start, so the deterministic number of holders stops increasing after 1 adoption.
- Question 2: the first event is an adoption with probability `2×3 / (2×3 + 4) = 3/5`; the belief is never passed on with probability 2/5. With two people unaware, a stop has probability `4 / (2×2 + 4) = 1/2`, so exactly 2 people ever hold it with probability `3/5 × 1/2 × 1/2 = 3/20`.
- Question 3: final size 1 with probability 2/5 = 0.4, 2 with 3/20 = 0.15, 3 with 7/45 = 0.1556, 4 with 53/180 = 0.2944. Mean 211/90 = 2.34. Most likely value 1, least likely 2. Two humps (at 1 and at 4, with the valley at 2).
- Question 4: z = 0.8678, that is 3.47 people. A run ends at 3 or 4 with probability 9/20 = 0.45. At most 2 people ever hold the belief with probability 11/20 = 0.55, against a branching value of 1/R0 = 1/2. With 3 initial holders the branching value is (1/2)³ = 1/8 = 0.125 and the exact probability that the fourth person never adopts is (2/3)³ = 8/27 = 0.2963: the branching value assumes that every conversation reaches somebody unaware, while here only one person of four is left to convince, so each holder is more likely to stop first (2/3) than in an unlimited population.
- Question 5 has no single answer. A sufficient plan keeps, for each belief and each person, who holds it, what it says, from whom it came, when it was adopted, when the person stopped bringing it up, and who was exposed without adopting; fixes in advance one result that counts against contagion (for example, most adopters name the same announcement or the same person as their source, which is a broadcast and not a chain); and says that one team and one belief give a single draw from a two-humped distribution, so that neither "everyone adopted it" nor "nobody did" estimates R0 or supports "nearly everyone will".

Score five criteria 0–2: (1) R0, the threshold and the number of adoptions before holders stop rising; (2) the three jump-chain probabilities; (3) the full distribution, its mean and its two humps, with the mean identified as an unlikely outcome; (4) the deterministic size set against the distribution, and the two comparisons with the branching value, including the reason for the gap with 3 initial holders; (5) a record and a falsifier fixed in advance, with the hypothetical team kept apart from Streeling evidence.
