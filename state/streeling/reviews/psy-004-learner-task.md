# PSY-004 learner task — no answer key

You have read **How Large Is Large? Aggregate Predictability with Fourteen Agents**. Complete this transfer task without opening the course answer blocks. The review board below is hypothetical: it is not Streeling data and not a forecast.

A product team sends every release proposal to a board of 20 reviewers. Each reviewer answers approve or reject, and over many proposals each reviewer approves 80% of the time. For each proposal the team reports the share of approvals, and it wants that share to have a standard deviation of at most 0.02 around the long-run rate.

1. Assume that the 20 reviewers answer independently. Compute the standard deviation of the share of approvals for one proposal, and the number of independent reviewers that would bring it down to 0.02. Then suppose that all 20 approve one proposal: give the lower bound of the exact 95% interval for a reviewer's approval probability.
2. The reviewers all read the same briefing note before they answer, and the answers of any two reviewers turn out to have a correlation of 0.25. Compute the design effect, the effective sample size of the board of 20 and the standard deviation of the share.
3. The team proposes to enlarge the board to 200 reviewers, then to 2,000, all reading the same note. Compute the effective sample size and the standard deviation of the share for both boards, and the limits that the two quantities approach as the board grows. Can a larger board reach the 0.02 target? State the criterion you used.
4. Model the note as the lesson models a shared signal: the note recommends approval with probability 0.8; each reviewer follows the note with probability c and otherwise decides alone, approving with probability 0.8. Find the c that gives a correlation of 0.25, then a reviewer's approval probability when the note recommends approval and when it recommends rejection. Compute the probability that all 20 approve, first for independent reviewers and then with the shared note, and the ratio of the two.
5. The team claims that "20 approvals out of 20 show that the proposal is sound". Write a plan, fixed before any new data arrive, to find out how far the board's answers can be treated as independent: what to record, which quantity to estimate, and one result that would falsify the claim that the reviewers answer independently. Say what your plan cannot establish.

Return your calculations, your plan and your uncertainty. Write "not assessed" where evidence is absent. The reviewer scores the unedited response separately; this task contains no answers.
