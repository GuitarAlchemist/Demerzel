## Answer-free learner trial

Give [`psy-017-learner-task.md`](psy-017-learner-task.md) without this brief or the course answer blocks. Record prior experience with probability and forecasting, elapsed time, the unedited response, confusion, and any lesson revision followed by retest.

Expected answers, copied from `results.json` under `transfer_task` (a hypothetical incident-review log, computed from `scenarios.json`):

- Question 1: the probability of 3 or more overturned among 10 is 0.070191 at a true rate of 10% and 0.011504 at 5%. 10% is inside the exact 95% interval (0.070191 is above 0.025) and 5% is outside (0.011504 is below 0.025). The interval for 3 of 10 runs from 0.0667 to 0.6525.
- Question 2: exactly 3 of 10 has probability 0.266828 at 30% and 0.057396 at 10%, a ratio of 4.6489. The count is between four and five times as probable at 30%; the ratio compares two chosen rates and says nothing about the rates in between or about whether "overturned" means "wrong".
- Question 3: c = 4, with a false alarm of 0.012795 ("3 or more" would give 0.070191, above the limit). The rule detects a true rate of 30% with probability 0.350389 and misses it with probability 0.649611. The observed count, 3, does not trigger it.
- Question 4: the nudge is 0 for 0 or 1 overturned (a share of 0.10 does not exceed 0.10), −0.04 for 2 and −0.05 for 3 or more. A calibrated team is nudged in one batch with probability 0.263901, at least once in 4 batches with probability 0.706408 and in 13 batches with probability 0.981372. The expected nudge per batch is −0.011258, so the expected total is −0.045 after 4 batches and −0.1464 after 13: the rule lowers the allowed confidence of a calibrated team, because nothing moves it up.
- Question 5 has no single answer. A sufficient plan fixes, before any new diagnosis is judged, what counts as a confident diagnosis and as overturned, the delay after which a diagnosis is judged, the rule "c or more of n" with its false-alarm and miss rates, and a falsifier (for example, c or more overturned among the next n diagnoses judged at that delay). It says that the ten diagnoses already seen cannot settle the question and that an overturned diagnosis is not necessarily a wrong one. Reference from the fixture (`q5_reference_sample`): separating 10% from 30% with a false alarm of at most 5% and a detection of at least 80% needs 25 diagnoses ("6 or more of 25": 0.0334 and 0.806512; 28 fails again and every sample from 29 on passes).

Score five criteria 0–2: (1) the two tail probabilities and the reading of the interval for 10% and 5%; (2) the likelihood ratio, with what it does not show; (3) the decision rule: critical count, false alarm and miss rate; (4) the correction rule: nudge by count, probability per batch and over several batches, and the one-sided drift named; (5) a plan fixed in advance with a falsifier, and the hypothetical log kept apart from Streeling evidence.
